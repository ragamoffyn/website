AI & UX

AI is changing a significant portion of the UX process, and keeping pace with it takes active effort. I pay particular attention to the areas below — both as a practitioner using AI tools in my own work, and as a designer thinking about what it means to design products that incorporate AI.

AI-Assisted Research

There is real value in using AI to support qualitative synthesis — surfacing themes across transcripts, comparing findings across sessions, flagging gaps in coverage. What still requires a trained researcher is judging what those patterns mean and whether they hold up against what was actually observed.

AI-Augmented Design

Generative tools can accelerate ideation considerably. Where they are less reliable is in decisions that depend on real understanding of a user's context. AI produces variations quickly; deciding which are appropriate still takes someone familiar with the work the users are actually doing.

Designing AI Experiences

Designing AI-powered features carries specific UX challenges, largely because the system's behavior isn't fully predictable. That reshapes how you think about trust, feedback, and error handling — users need enough transparency to calibrate their confidence without that transparency becoming its own cognitive burden.
