About

Where cognitive science meets complex design

My foundation is a Ph.D. in cognitive psychology — the study of how people learn, decide, and fail under pressure. I spent years in research settings asking what mental models people actually build, what information overload really costs them, and where human judgment breaks down. That grounding shapes everything I design.

What followed was a career that refused to stay in one lane. I've done UX for military decision support systems, EU-funded crisis management platforms, air traffic control, precision agriculture, offshore energy operations, financial innovation labs, and mobile games. Each domain different. The challenge always the same: helping skilled professionals make better decisions under pressure, with tools that get out of the way.

I'm drawn to environments where the stakes are real and the users are experts. Expert users don't need hand-holding — they need interfaces that respect their mental models, surface the right information at the right moment, and never add cognitive load where none is warranted. Getting that right requires both rigorous research and informed design intuition. I work at that intersection.

Currently based in Oslo, leading UX for a digital solutions platform in the offshore energy sector. The work involves building a design system, embedding research across product teams, and untangling the inconsistencies that accumulate when software grows faster than its UX.

Context

4Subsea builds monitoring and integrity management software for offshore wind and oil and gas operations — data-intensive tools used by engineers in safety-critical contexts. As the sole UX practitioner, I'm responsible for the full UX of the digital solutions platform: how products relate, behave, and evolve together.

Challenge

The platform had grown organically. Inconsistent interaction patterns, terminology, and navigation models had accumulated between products. Users switching between tools encountered unnecessary cognitive friction — the last thing you want for engineers managing offshore assets. The company was also expanding its user base and needed the platform to project maturity and coherence.

Constraints

Solo practitioner in an engineering-led organization. Users are expert offshore engineers and analysts — high domain expertise, low patience for UI that slows them down. Formative research had to be efficient and embedded in existing workflows rather than extractive.

What I Did

  • Adopted and refined the company's first design system — shared components, patterns, documentation — bringing consistency across products.
  • Led a platform-level usability redesign: auditing products, improving navigation models, information hierarchy, and data display conventions.
  • Embedded user research across product teams, making research a shared practice rather than a siloed function.

What I Learned

Building a design system through organizational buy-in requires as much communication design as UI design. And expert users have more tolerance for complexity than for inconsistency — they'll navigate a dense interface if the logic is coherent; they won't forgive arbitrary differences between products they rely on daily.
